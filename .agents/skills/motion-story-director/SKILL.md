# Motion Story Director

Treat the video as a chain of **visible causes and consequences**. A polished illustration, camera zoom, parallax, floating particles, or fade can support a scene; none alone counts as the action that explains its line of narration.

## Before production

1. Read the project's current brief, quality standard, and decisions. When references exist, inspect short moving sequences as well as stills. Record what is directly observed, what the user said, and what remains inference. Extract principles; never copy a reference's assets, characters, composition, or interface.
2. Make a visual passport for the topic: style, material, color contrast, light, camera, typography, subtitle behavior, transitions, and two or three topic-specific visual devices. Avoid importing an earlier video's complete look.
3. For each narration beat, write **before → cause → action/reaction → after**. Name the layers required to show it, the viewpoint, text's role, and the transition to the next beat. A scene described only as “show image + pan/zoom” must be redesigned before asset generation.

## Direct the motion

- Keep a recognizable object, direction, color, or trajectory across a scale change. Make the camera reveal a mechanism or follow an action; do not use camera motion as a substitute for the mechanism.
- Choose the rendering method by the action: editable shapes for diagrams, separated image layers for illustrated environments, rigged/posed parts for a necessary character, and compositing/3D for spatial transformations or difficult organic motion. Decide between Remotion and After Effects from a short test of the hardest scene, not from tool preference.
- Give text one role at a time: verbatim subtitle, kinetic thesis/number, or label attached to an object. Plan entrance, assembly, hold, and exit on the audio timeline. Let emphasis coincide with an observable change. Avoid a repeated entrance preset and competing duplicate captions.
- A character is useful when gesture, gaze, expression, or interaction advances the cause-and-effect chain. Do not add a mascot merely to fill space. Respect any project decision to include or exclude one.
- Sound accents should coincide with events such as impact, switch, morph, count, or revealed conclusion. Preserve voice clarity.

## Proof and review

Make a small animatic and a 3–8 second moving proof of the riskiest scene, including its transition and typography, before building every scene. Review the proof without audio: can the viewer identify the cause and result? Inspect at phone size and at full size. Iterate on the action, not merely the background treatment.

Before calling a render review-ready, watch the whole encoded video in motion using the silent copy. Verify the audio file, mix levels and timing technically; never play audible media on the laptop. Check every text entrance/exit, scene seam, direction continuity, asset edges, subtitle timing, first/last frame, and any stretch where several seconds pass without a new meaningful visual event. A technically valid MP4 is not creative acceptance.

Use a tool skill only for the actual technique: the installed technique-specific skill if available; otherwise use the chosen tool's official documentation. The bundled templates/remotion foundation does not require global skills. Never claim a missing tool is installed. Follow the project's own documents for its format, voice, approval stages, and character decision.
